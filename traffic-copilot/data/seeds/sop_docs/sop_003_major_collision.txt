STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE
SOP-003: MAJOR COLLISION RESPONSE
Traffic Management Center — Version 4.0 | Effective: 2025-06-01
Review Cycle: Annual | Owner: Operations Division / Emergency Liaison

PURPOSE
This procedure governs the Traffic Management Center's role in coordinating the immediate and sustained response to a major collision event involving multiple vehicles, injuries, or significant infrastructure impact.

1. IMMEDIATE RESPONSE (0–5 MINUTES)
Upon notification of a major collision (defined as: 2+ vehicles involved, confirmed or suspected injuries, or one or more lanes blocked):

a) TMO acknowledges incident in IMS and assigns severity tier (1 = critical, 2 = serious, 3 = minor).
b) Activate nearest upstream VMS boards with "ACCIDENT AHEAD — SLOW DOWN" or equivalent.
c) Notify Duty Supervisor immediately for Tier 1 and 2 incidents.
d) Confirm emergency services (Police, Fire, Ambulance) have been dispatched via CAD integration or direct liaison with Emergency Communications (ext. 911 liaison line).
e) Begin continuous camera monitoring of the incident location and upstream/downstream segments.
f) Lock affected corridor segments in the Traffic State Engine to prevent automated plan changes.

2. RESOURCE DEPLOYMENT (5–15 MINUTES)
a) Dispatch a Traffic Incident Response (TIR) vehicle if not already on scene, prioritising arrival within 10 minutes.
b) Request additional field units if the incident is confirmed Tier 1 (major injury/fatality or multi-lane blockage exceeding 500 m queue).
c) If heavy recovery is required, contact the on-call towing contractor (details in Operations Contact Register) and confirm ETA.
d) Position shadow vehicles at the head of the queue (200 m upstream) and at diversion entry points.
e) Notify utility companies if infrastructure damage is suspected (e.g., barrier strike, fuel spill, overhead line damage).

3. CORRIDOR MANAGEMENT
a) Implement lane closure protocol per SOP-001 for all blocked lanes.
b) Activate diversion route per SOP-002 if one or more lanes blocked for expected duration > 20 minutes.
c) Coordinate signal retiming per SOP-004 to optimise diversion route and downstream clearance.
d) Manage upstream entry ramps: consider ramp metering or temporary ramp closure if queue exceeds 5 km.
e) Monitor secondary corridors for overspill congestion every 10 minutes and report to Duty Supervisor.

4. COORDINATION WITH EMERGENCY SERVICES
a) Establish and maintain direct radio contact with the Police incident commander on dedicated inter-agency channel.
b) Do not reopen lanes until explicit clearance is received from Police incident commander.
c) Provide EMS with signal priority corridors if hospital route optimisation is requested (coordinate with Signal Plans team).
d) Forward real-time queue and speed data to the Fire Incident Commander if fuel spill or fire risk is active.
e) Log all inter-agency communications in IMS with timestamp and agency identifier.

5. STAND-DOWN AND RECOVERY
Tier 1 incidents require a post-incident debrief within 48 hours. Incident report must include: timeline of TMC actions, VMS/diversion activation log, resource deployment log, and lessons learned. Submit to Operations Manager and Emergency Liaison within 5 working days.
