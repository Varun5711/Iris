STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE
SOP-002: TRAFFIC DIVERSION ROUTE ACTIVATION
Traffic Management Center — Version 3.1 | Effective: 2025-03-01
Review Cycle: Annual | Owner: Operations Division

PURPOSE
This procedure defines the conditions, sequence of actions, and responsibilities for activating a pre-approved diversion route when primary corridor capacity is critically degraded.

1. DIVERSION TRIGGER CONDITIONS
A diversion route shall be activated when any of the following thresholds are met:

a) Primary corridor speed has dropped below 20 km/h for more than 5 consecutive minutes.
b) A full carriageway closure has been confirmed (see SOP-001).
c) Predicted queue length on the primary corridor exceeds 3 km, as estimated by the Traffic State Engine.
d) Emergency Services have requested corridor clearance for critical response operations.
e) A structural or safety hazard has been declared by an authorised engineer or field commander.

2. ROUTE ASSESSMENT
Before activating, the TMO must verify:
a) The designated diversion route (listed in the Corridor Diversion Register, CDR) is passable — no conflicting closures or known hazards on the alternate.
b) The diversion route can absorb the expected diverted volume without exceeding Level of Service D. Cross-reference the CDR capacity ratings.
c) Oversize load or hazardous goods vehicles are assessed separately; standard diversion routes may be unsuitable. Notify freight desk (ext. 205) if applicable.
d) Confirm with field unit or camera monitoring that diversion entry/exit points are clear and visible.

3. SIGNAL TIMING COORDINATION
Upon diversion activation, the TMO must immediately:
a) Request signal retiming on the diversion route from the Signal Plans team (see SOP-004).
b) Key intersections on the diversion route must have green phase extended by a minimum of 15 seconds for critical approaches within 10 minutes of activation.
c) Coordinate with the upstream entry intersection to introduce metering if diversion flow exceeds 80% of alternate route capacity.
d) Monitor diversion route speeds via sensor feed; escalate to Duty Supervisor if secondary congestion develops above Level of Service E.

4. PUBLIC NOTIFICATION SEQUENCE
Notifications must be issued in the following order and within the stated timeframes:

Step 1 (within 3 minutes): Activate VMS boards on the primary corridor upstream of the closure/incident. Display diversion instruction.
Step 2 (within 5 minutes): Activate VMS boards at key decision points on feeder roads directing drivers to the diversion route.
Step 3 (within 8 minutes): Issue radio broadcast to participating stations (see SOP-005).
Step 4 (within 10 minutes): Publish social media advisory via the approved TMC account (see SOP-005).
Step 5 (within 15 minutes): Notify the regional 511 travel information service via the automated data feed. Confirm feed update with 511 operator desk if automated feed is unavailable.

5. DEACTIVATION
Diversion shall be deactivated only after the primary corridor has been confirmed clear, queues have substantially dissipated (speed > 55 km/h on primary segment), and field unit confirms safe conditions. Remove VMS and update 511 within 5 minutes of deactivation. Log all times in IMS.
